# Celtic Mythology

Source: mythologies.wiki (182 entries), ACP (13 Tuatha Dé Danann), comparative sources

## Key Themes
- Otherworld (Tir na nOg, Annwn) - realm of gods/fairies
- Sacred kingship and sovereignty
- Shape-shifting
- Geasa (taboos/obligations)
- Hero's taboos and fate
- Tripartism (threefold aspects)
- Head cult (head as seat of soul)
- Seasonal festivals (Samhain, Imbolc, Beltane, Lughnasadh)

## Pantheon Structure
**Tuatha Dé Danann (Irish):**
- Dagda (father god, cauldron, club)
- Lugh (many-skilled, sun/light, harvest)
- Morrigan (war, fate, sovereignty - triple goddess)
- Brigid (fire, poetry, healing, smithcraft)
- Nuada (king, silver hand)
- Ogma (eloquence, writing)
- Dian Cecht (healing)
- Goibniu (smith)
- Lir (sea)
- Manannán mac Lir (Otherworld, mists)
- Aengus (love, youth)
- Bodb Derg, Midir, etc.

**Welsh (Mabinogion):**
- Lleu Llaw Gyffes
- Rhiannon (horse, sovereignty)
- Pwyll, Manawydan
- Bran the Blessed
- Arawn (Otherworld king)
- Ceridwen (cauldron of inspiration)

## Notable Creatures
- Banshee (death omen)
- Púca (shapeshifter)
- Selkies (seal-folk)
- Kelpie (water horse)
- Faeries (Aos Sí)
- Dragons/Wyrms
- Cauldron of Plenty/Rebirth

## Shared Motifs
- Flood: Not central; various local flood myths
- Dying God: Not central; seasonal cycles
- World Tree: Not prominent; sacred trees (oak, ash, yew)
- Trickster: Not pure; Lugh (skill), Aengus (love tricks)
- Underworld Journey: Arthurian Annwn, Pwyll/Arawn exchange

## Game Design Notes
- Geasa as narrative constraints/mechanics
- Otherworld access at liminal times/places
- Sovereignty goddess as land legitimacy
- Tripartite deities (Maiden/Mother/Crone or functional triads)
- Head-taking as power transfer
- Seasonal calendar driving events
