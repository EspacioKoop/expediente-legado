# Mesopotamian Mythology

Source: mythologies.wiki (126 entries), ACP (13 Sumerian/Babylonian), comparative sources

## Key Themes
- Creation from conflict (Marduk kills Tiamat)
- Flood narrative (Utnapishtim in Epic of Gilgamesh)
- Gilgamesh quest for immortality
- Descent to underworld (Inanna)
- Me (divine powers/civilization gifts)
- Divine assembly (Anunnaki)
- Kingship as divine mandate
- Humanity created to serve gods

## Pantheon Structure
**Sumerian/Babylonian/Assyrian:**
- Anu (sky father, supreme)
- Enlil (air/wind, king of gods, fate decrees)
- Enki/Ea (wisdom, water, magic, creator of humans)
- Ninhursag (mother goddess, birth)
- Inanna/Ishtar (love, war, Venus, descent)
- Utu/Shamash (sun, justice)
- Nanna/Sin (moon)
- Ereshkigal (underworld queen)
- Marduk (Babylonian chief, slays Tiamat)
- Nabu (writing, wisdom)
- Ashur (Assyrian national god)

## Notable Creatures
- Tiamat (primordial chaos dragon/sea mother)
- Mushussu (dragon of Marduk)
- Lamassu (protective deity, human-headed bull/lion)
- Pazuzu (demon king of winds)
- Anzu (lion-headed eagle, stole tablet of destinies)
- Scorpion-men (gatekeepers of sun)
- Bull of Heaven

## Shared Motifs
- Flood: Utnapishtim warned by Enki, builds boat (direct ancestor of Noah)
- Dying God: Dumuzi/Tammuz (Inanna's consort, annual death/return)
- World Tree: Not prominent
- Trickster: Enki (clever, helps humanity), sometimes Inanna
- Underworld Journey: Inanna's descent (7 gates, dies, resurrected)
