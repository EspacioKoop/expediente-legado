# Polynesian Mythology

Source: mythologies.wiki (65 entries), ACP (17 Polynesian), comparative sources

## Key Themes
- Mana (spiritual power/authority)
- Tapu (sacred restriction/prohibition)
- Ancestral homeland (Hawaiki)
- Oceanic voyaging and navigation
- Genealogy (whakapapa) as sacred knowledge
- Trickster hero Māui
- Sky-earth separation

## Pantheon Structure
**Major Deities (varies by island group):**
- Io (supreme, abstract - Māori)
- Tangaroa (sea, fish)
- Tāne (forests, birds, humans)
- Rongo (peace, cultivated foods)
- Tū (war)
- Haumia-tiketike (wild foods)
- Whiro (darkness, evil)
- Māui (demi-god, trickster, culture hero)
- Hina (moon, women)
- Pele (volcanoes, fire - Hawaiian)
- Lono (fertility, peace - Hawaiian)
- Kāne (creation, life - Hawaiian)
- Kanaloa (ocean, underworld - Hawaiian)

## Notable Creatures
- Taniwha (water spirits/guardians)
- Mo'o (lizard guardians)
- Patupaiarehe (fairy folk)
- Menehune (little people - Hawaiian)
- Sharks as ancestral guardians

## Shared Motifs
- Flood: Not central; sea level rise myths
- Dying God: Not central
- World Tree: Not prominent; cosmic separation
- Trickster: Māui (slows sun, fishes islands, steals fire)
- Underworld Journey: Various (Māui visits Hine-nui-te-pō)
