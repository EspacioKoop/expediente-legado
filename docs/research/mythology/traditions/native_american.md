# Native American Mythology

Source: mythologies.wiki, ACP (14 Native American traditions), comparative sources

## Key Themes
- Deep connection to land and place
- Animal spirits and totems
- Trickster figures (Coyote, Raven, Hare, Spider)
- Emergence stories
- Sacred ceremonies and cycles
- Oral tradition as living knowledge
- Diversity: hundreds of distinct nations with unique traditions

## Major Cultural Regions & Traditions
**Eastern Woodlands:**
- Iroquois (Haudenosaunee): Sky Woman, Great Peacemaker, Three Sisters
- Algonquian: Wisakedjak (trickster), Windigo
- Southeastern: Corn Mother, Feathered Serpent parallels

**Plains:**
- Lakota/Dakota: White Buffalo Calf Woman, Thunder Beings, Sun Dance
- Blackfoot: Napi (Old Man, trickster-cultural hero)
- Cheyenne: Maheo (Creator), Sweet Medicine

**Southwest:**
- Pueblo (Hopi, Zuni): Kachinas, Emergence, Spider Grandmother
- Navajo (Diné): Changing Woman, Monster Slayers, Coyote
- Apache: White Painted Woman, Child of Water

**Pacific Northwest:**
- Raven (transformer-trickster), Salmon people, Totem poles
- Thunderbird, Kolus (younger thunder brother)
- Raven Steals the Light

**Arctic/Subarctic:**
- Inuit/Sedna (sea goddess), Northern Lights legends
- Shamans, animal spirits
- Yup'ik: Raven, Fox, tricky spirits

**Mesoamerican Overlap** (covered separately): Aztec, Maya, Olmec, etc.

## Shared Motifs (Pan-Indigenous)
- Flood: Many tribes have flood survival stories (often with boat or raft)
- Dying God: Not central; transformation, seasonal cycles
- World Tree: Axis mundi (tree connecting worlds), sometimes specific (cedar, pine)
- Trickster: Extremely common (Coyote, Raven, Hare, Mink, Spider)
- Underworld Journey: Less emphasized; focus on sky world or earth renewal

## Game Design Notes
- **Land-based magic**: Powers tied to specific places, plants, animals
- **Animal allies/spirits**: Totems as companions or guides
- **Trickster as mentor or challenge**: Tests humility, teaches through failure
- **Ceremony as power**: Proper ritual maintains balance and invokes aid
- **Oral tradition**: Knowledge passed through story, song, dance
- **Diversity requirement**: Avoid pan-Indian stereotypes; specify nations
- **Respect**: Many traditions are living and protected; consult appropriate cultural authorities

## Important Concepts
- **Orenda/Iya**: Spiritual power (Iroquois concept)
- **Manitou**: Spirit, essence (Algonquian)
- **Wakȟáŋ**: Sacred, mysterious (Lakota)
- **Haiyupis**: Spirit world (Nuu-chah-nulth)
