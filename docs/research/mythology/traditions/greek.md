# Greek Mythology

Source: mythologies.wiki (735 entries), ACP (22 Olympians), comparative sources

## Key Themes
- Divine succession and overthrow (Uranus → Cronus → Zeus)
- Hubris and nemesis (excessive pride leads to downfall)
- Fate vs free will (Moirae, prophecies)
- Xenia (guest-host relationship)
- Metamorphosis (transformations by gods)
- Nostos (heroic return home)

## Pantheon Structure
**Olympians** (12 major + others):
- Zeus (sky, thunder, king)
- Hera (marriage, women, queen)
- Poseidon (sea, earthquakes, horses)
- Demeter (agriculture, grain)
- Athena (wisdom, war, crafts)
- Apollo (sun, light, music, prophecy)
- Artemis (moon, hunt, wilderness)
- Ares (war, violence)
- Aphrodite (love, beauty, desire)
- Hephaestus (fire, forge, crafts)
- Hermes (messenger, travel, thieves)
- Dionysus (wine, ecstasy, theater)

**Primordials & Titans**:
- Gaia (Earth), Uranus (Sky), Cronus (Time), Rhea
- Oceanus, Hyperion, Iapetus, etc.

**Chthonic & Others**:
- Hades/Persephone (underworld)
- Hestia (hearth)
- Pan (nature, shepherds)
- Muses, Graces, Fates (Moirae), Furies (Erinyes)

## Notable Creatures
- Pegasus (winged horse)
- Chimera (lion-goat-snake)
- Medusa (Gorgon)
- Minotaur (bull-man)
- Hydra (multi-headed serpent)
- Cerberus (three-headed dog)
- Sphinx (lion-woman, riddles)
- Harpies (wind spirits)
- Centaurs (horse-man)
- Satyrs (goat-man, Dionysus)

## Hero Archetypes
- Heracles (strength, labors)
- Perseus (monster slayer)
- Theseus (founder, Minotaur)
- Odysseus (cunning, nostos)
- Achilles (warrior, heel)
- Jason (Argo, Golden Fleece)
- Orpheus (musician, underworld)

## Shared Motifs
- Flood: Deucalion & Pyrrha (parallel to Noah/Utnapishtim)
- Dying God: Dionysus (dies and reborn annually)
- World Tree: Not prominent; Olympus as mountain
- Trickster: Hermes (infant theft of cattle, messenger)
- Underworld Journey: Orpheus, Heracles, Theseus, Odysseus

## Game Design Notes
- Olympus reflects hierarchical monarchy with familial drama
- Gods intervene in mortal affairs capriciously
- Heroes often favored by one god, persecuted by another
- Myths explain natural phenomena, constellations, city foundations
- Cult worship varied by city (Athena-Athens, Poseidon-Corinth, etc.)
