# Australian Aboriginal Mythology

Source: mythologies.wiki (119 entries), ACP (8 Dreamtime), comparative sources

## Key Themes
- The Dreaming (Tjukurpa/Jukurrpa) - eternal present, not past
- Songlines (dreaming tracks) map sacred geography
- Land as sacred text and law
- Totemism (kin linked to species/place)
- Ancestral beings shaped landscape
- Ceremonial law maintains cosmic order
- 50,000+ years continuous tradition
- Over 250 language groups

## Key Concepts
- **Dreaming**: Ancestral creation period that is also eternal now
- **Songlines**: Paths across land/sky, encoded in song/dance/painting
- **Totem**: Species/place linked to person/clan; carries responsibility
- **Law**: Ceremonial knowledge, not written; maintained through initiation
- **Country**: Land as living entity with which people have reciprocal relationship

## Notable Ancestral Beings
- Rainbow Serpent (creator, water, fertility)
- Baiame (sky father, law-giver)
- Wondjina (cloud/spirit beings, rock art)
- Mimi spirits (thin, rock art creators)
- Seven Sisters (Pleiades, chased across sky)
- Emu in the Sky (dark constellation)
- Crow, Eaglehawk (moiety totems)

## Shared Motifs
- Flood: Some coastal groups have sea-level rise stories
- Dying God: Not central; transformation into landscape
- World Tree: Not prominent; specific sacred trees/sites
- Trickster: Crow, various culture heroes
- Underworld Journey: Not emphasized; spirit returns to Country

## Game Design Notes
- **Land as character**: Country has agency, memory, needs
- **Songlines as navigation/quest lines**: Knowledge = movement
- **Totemic responsibility**: Player linked to species/place duties
- **Ceremony as mechanic**: Ritual maintains world state
- **Elders as knowledge gates**: Initiation unlocks content
- **No "history" vs "myth"**: Dreaming is present reality
- **Respectful adaptation**: Living culture, consult Traditional Owners

## Cultural Protocols
- Secret/sacred knowledge not for public use
- Gender-restricted knowledge (men's/women's business)
- Clan-specific stories not transferable
- Always attribute to specific language group/nation
