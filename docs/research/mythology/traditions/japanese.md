# Japanese Mythology

Source: mythologies.wiki (259 entries), ACP (17 Kami), comparative sources

## Key Themes
- Kami (divine spirits) in all natural phenomena
- Purity (kegare) vs pollution
- Imperial descent from Amaterasu
- Shinto + Buddhist syncretism
- Ancestor veneration
- Seasonal festivals (matsuri)
- Boundary/threshold sacredness

## Pantheon Structure
**Kami of Creation:**
- Ame-no-Minakanushi (central master)
- Takamimusubi, Kamimusubi (creative powers)

**Heavenly Kami (Amatsukami):**
- Amaterasu (sun goddess, imperial ancestor)
- Tsukuyomi (moon god)
- Susanoo (storm god, trickster)
- Ame-no-Uzume (dawn, mirth, dance)
- Ninigi (Amaterasu's grandson, sent to rule earth)

**Earthly Kami (Kunitsukami):**
- Ōkuninushi (land master, medicine)
- Sarutahiko (guide, crossroads)
- Inari (rice, foxes, prosperity)

**Buddhist-Origin/Shared:**
- Hachiman (war, archery)
- Benzaiten (water, arts)
- Daikokuten (wealth)
- Jizō (protector of children, travelers)

## Notable Creatures
- Kitsune (fox spirits, Inari messengers)
- Tanuki (raccoon dog, shapeshifters)
- Tengu (bird-like mountain spirits)
- Oni (ogres/demons)
- Kappa (water imps)
- Yōkai (supernatural beings)
- Ryū (dragons)
- Baku (dream eaters)

## Shared Motifs
- Flood: Not central; Izanagi/Izanami stir chaos
- Dying God: Not central; Susanoo's redemption arc
- World Tree: Not prominent; Takamagahara (high plain of heaven)
- Trickster: Susanoo (chaos → hero), Fox spirits
- Underworld Journey: Izanagi visits Yomi (land of dead)
